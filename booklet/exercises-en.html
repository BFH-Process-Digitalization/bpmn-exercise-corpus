T1-B Tyre change at a garage

Topic 1: Activities, Basic | Time: 10 minutes, Core exercise

Task

A garage replaces its customers' summer wheels with winter wheels in autumn. The process starts when a customer arrives with her car for the agreed appointment. The service advisor takes over the car. A mechanic then fits the winter wheels and checks the tyre pressure. After that he puts the summer wheels into wheel storage. Finally the service advisor creates the invoice and hands the car back to the customer. This completes the tyre change. Model the process.

Learning goal

Model a linear process with start and end event; name activities as object and verb.

Sample solution: T1-B

Typical mistakes

  • Activities named with a noun only (e.g. "Invoice").
  • End event missing or named as an activity.
  • Roles (advisor, mechanic) modelled as activities.

T1-B2 Goods receipt at a distribution centre

Topic 1: Activities, Basic | Time: 10 minutes, Core exercise

Task

At a retailer's distribution centre, deliveries arrive every day. As soon as a truck is at the loading dock, a warehouse employee checks the delivery note. He then unloads the pallets and inspects a sample of the goods. Next he posts the goods receipt in the warehouse management system and puts the goods away. The process ends when the goods are stored. Model the process.

Learning goal

Model a linear process from the triggering event to the result.

Sample solution: T1-B2

Typical mistakes

  • Start event named as an activity instead of a state.
  • Two steps merged into one activity ("Unload and inspect pallets").
  • Order of posting and storing swapped.

T1-B3 Maintenance of a CNC milling machine

Topic 1: Activities, Basic | Time: 10 minutes

Task

A supplier to the watch industry maintains its CNC milling machines itself. When the maintenance team receives a maintenance order, a technician shuts down the machine. She changes the coolant and then lubricates the guideways. After that she runs a test cycle and then releases the machine for production. The process ends when the machine is ready for operation again. Model the process.

Learning goal

Break a technical procedure into clearly named single steps.

Sample solution: T1-B3

Typical mistakes

  • Result modelled as an activity instead of an end event.
  • Inconsistent terms for the same thing ("stop machine", "shut down plant").
  • Start event missing.

T1-A Calibration order for an industrial scale

Topic 1: Activities, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A manufacturer of industrial scales calibrates scales at its customers' sites. The process starts when a customer sends a calibration order. The CRM system loads the customer data automatically. The price follows from the rate table by scale type and measuring range. A dispatcher sets an appointment in the planning system; the manufacturer then sends the appointment confirmation to the customer. A service technician calibrates the scale on site by hand, without IT support. A script creates the calibration certificate automatically from the measurements. The manufacturer sends the certificate and invoice to the customer and then waits for the payment. When the payment has arrived, the order is closed. Model the manufacturer's process with the matching task types; the customer is a collapsed pool.

Learning goal

Choose the matching task type: user, service, business rule, send, receive, manual and script task.

Sample solution: T1-A

Typical mistakes

  • All activities as plain tasks without a type.
  • Determining the price as a user task instead of a business rule task.
  • Waiting for payment as a user task instead of a receive task.

T1-A2 Commissioning a filling line at the customer

Topic 1: Activities, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A manufacturer of filling lines commissions its lines at the customer's plant. The process starts when the line has been delivered to the customer. First the assembly team assembles the line: it builds the mechanics, connects the electrics and configures the control system. A safety engineer then carries out the safety check. The safety check is a separate company-wide process that other processes use as well; its steps are not modelled here. The team then trains the operators and has the customer sign the acceptance report. The line is then handed over. Model the assembly as an expanded subprocess.

Learning goal

Structure a process with a subprocess and include a reused process as a call activity.

Sample solution: T1-A2

Typical mistakes

  • Safety check as an ordinary subprocess instead of a call activity.
  • Assembly steps put directly into the main process without a subprocess.
  • Subprocess without its own start and end event.

T1-A3 Launching a new generation of hearing aids

Topic 1: Activities, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A hearing system manufacturer launches a new generation of devices. After the launch decision, marketing creates the promotional material: it writes the brochure, takes product photos and shoots a product video, in any order and as needed. Sales then trains all distribution partners; the trainings in the individual countries run at the same time. Development then tests the fitting software until no more errors occur. Finally logistics supplies the pilot clinics, one after the other. The product is then launched. Model the process.

Learning goal

Tell an ad-hoc subprocess, a loop and parallel and sequential multi-instance apart.

Sample solution: T1-A3

Typical mistakes

  • Promotional material as an ordinary subprocess with a fixed order.
  • Trainings as a loop instead of parallel multi-instance.
  • Software test as multi-instance instead of a loop (the number of runs is not known in advance).

T2-B Service visit to a heat pump

Topic 2: Gateways and parallelism, Basic | Time: 10 minutes, Core exercise

Task

A building services company maintains heat pumps in private homes. After a service visit, the service technician records the work report. Dispatch then checks the warranty claim. If it is a warranty case, dispatch registers the work with the manufacturer for reimbursement. Otherwise it invoices the customer. In both cases dispatch then closes the order. Model the process.

Learning goal

Model an exclusive decision as an XOR gateway with a question and labelled outgoing flows, and merge it again.

Sample solution: T2-B

Typical mistakes

  • AND gateway instead of XOR: both paths would run.
  • Outgoing flows of the XOR gateway not labelled.
  • Paths not merged; "Close order" modelled twice.

T2-B2 Preparing a production order

Topic 2: Gateways and parallelism, Basic | Time: 10 minutes, Core exercise

Task

In an engineering company, production planning releases a production order. Three preparations then run at the same time: the warehouse picks the raw material, the workshop sets up the machine and quality assurance provides the inspection plan. Only when all three preparations are done does the workshop manufacture the parts. Quality assurance then inspects the parts. This completes the production order. Model the process. Roles do not need to be shown.

Learning goal

Split and synchronise concurrent work with AND gateways.

Sample solution: T2-B2

Typical mistakes

  • XOR gateway instead of AND: only one preparation would run.
  • Parallel paths joined without an AND join.
  • Preparations modelled in sequence instead of in parallel.

T2-B3 Shipping a spare part abroad

Topic 2: Gateways and parallelism, Basic | Time: 10 minutes

Task

A packaging machine manufacturer receives an order for a spare part from a customer abroad. The warehouse first checks availability. If the spare part is in stock, the warehouse picks it. Otherwise the warehouse has the spare part made in the company's own production. The warehouse then packs the spare part while the export department prepares the export documents at the same time. When both are done, the warehouse hands the shipment over to the carrier. The process ends when the spare part has been shipped. Model the process.

Learning goal

Tell XOR from AND in one process and close every gateway with its matching counterpart.

Sample solution: T2-B3

Typical mistakes

  • XOR paths not merged before the AND split.
  • XOR split closed with an AND join (or the other way round).
  • Export documents modelled after packing instead of in parallel.

T2-A Fixing defects after a building inspection

Topic 2: Gateways and parallelism, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A building services provider has installed electrical, ventilation and plumbing systems in an office building. At acceptance, the site manager inspects the building. Defects can occur in the electrics, the ventilation and the plumbing, in any combination. Each affected trade fixes its defects; the trades work at the same time. If there are no defects, this step is skipped. As soon as all commissioned trades are done, the site manager completes the acceptance report. The building is then handed over. Model the process.

Learning goal

Model a choice of one or more paths with an OR gateway and a default flow, and join it correctly.

Sample solution: T2-A

Typical mistakes

  • XOR gateway: only one trade could fix defects.
  • AND gateway: all three trades would always work.
  • OR split closed with an AND or XOR join (deadlock or duplicate execution).

T2-A2 Reacting to a quote

Topic 2: Gateways and parallelism, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A laboratory automation provider sends a customer a quote for a sample handling system. It then waits for the reaction. If the customer places the order, sales records the order. If the customer declines, sales records the reason. If there is no answer after 30 days, sales closes the quote as expired. Whichever event occurs first counts. Model the provider's process; the customer is a collapsed pool.

Learning goal

React to the first event that occurs with an event-based gateway.

Sample solution: T2-A2

Typical mistakes

  • XOR gateway with conditions instead of an event-based gateway.
  • Timer event missing, the process would wait forever.
  • Gateway labelled or its outgoing flows labelled yes/no.

T2-A3 Handling a cold chain shipment

Topic 2: Gateways and parallelism, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A pharmaceutical logistics company transports temperature-sensitive medicines. When a cold shipment has been registered, the warehouse activates the temperature logger and at the same time the customs department prepares the customs documents. Only when both are done does the driver transport the shipment. At the destination, quality assurance checks the temperature log. If a deviation was found, it informs the client about the deviation; otherwise it releases the shipment. Model the process. Model the decision after the check as a conditional flow with a default flow directly out of the check activity.

Learning goal

Synchronise parallel work and model conditional flows with a default flow out of an activity.

Sample solution: T2-A3

Typical mistakes

  • Transport starts after only one of the two preparations (XOR join instead of AND join).
  • Conditional flow without a default flow: without a deviation the process would stop.
  • Both outputs of the check as conditional flows with overlapping conditions.

T3-A Breakdown of a cable car

Topic 3: Events, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A cable car manufacturer services an aerial tramway. When a breakdown is reported, a service technician fixes the breakdown. If fixing takes longer than 30 minutes, the operations manager informs the passengers about the delay; the repair continues. If the breakdown has not been fixed after 2 hours, the repair is stopped and the operations manager starts the rescue of the passengers. If the breakdown is fixed in time, the technician puts the tramway back into operation. Model the process.

Learning goal

Use interrupting and non-interrupting timer boundary events for time limits.

Sample solution: T3-A

Typical mistakes

  • 30-minute event interrupting: the repair would be stopped.
  • 2-hour event non-interrupting: rescue and repair would run on together.
  • Time limits as timer intermediate events after the activity.

T3-A2 Testing and releasing a batch

Topic 3: Events, Advanced | Time: 15 minutes

Task

A food manufacturer fills infant formula. After a batch has been filled, the laboratory tests the batch microbiologically while quality assurance checks the labels at the same time. If germs are found, the laboratory announces the batch block to all distribution warehouses at once, without addressing a particular receiver, and discards the batch. This ends the whole process immediately, including a label check that is still running. If both checks have been completed without findings, quality assurance releases the batch. Model the process.

Learning goal

Broadcast a signal to all receivers and end all running paths with a terminate end event.

Sample solution: T3-A2

Typical mistakes

  • Message to one warehouse instead of a signal to all.
  • None end event: the label check would continue and the AND join would never complete.
  • AND join also receives the path with germs found.
